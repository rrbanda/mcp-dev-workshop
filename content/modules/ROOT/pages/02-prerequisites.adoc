= Prerequisites and Environment
:navtitle: Prerequisites and Environment

Everything is pre-configured. This page tells you what you need and how to
verify it works.

== What you need

[cols="1,2",options="header"]
|===
| Item | Details

| *Web browser*
| Chrome, Firefox, or Edge. Safari works but may have minor layout differences.

| *Workshop credentials card*
| Provided by the facilitator at the start of class. Contains: username,
  password, DevSpaces URL, and OpenCode Web password.

| *No local tooling*
| You do not need `oc`, Python, Docker, or any CLI on your laptop. Everything
  runs inside the DevSpaces workspace.
|===

== Verify your environment

=== Step 1: Log in to the OpenShift console

Open the console URL from your credentials card and log in with your username
and password.

Expected result: You see the OpenShift web console with your namespace listed.

=== Step 2: Open DevSpaces

Navigate to the DevSpaces dashboard:

----
{devspaces_url}
----

Or use the application launcher (grid icon, top-right) → *Red Hat OpenShift Dev Spaces*.

Expected result: The DevSpaces dashboard loads and shows your workspace.

=== Step 3: Start your workspace

If your workspace is stopped, click *Start*. Wait for it to reach the *Running*
state — this may take 1-2 minutes on first launch.

Expected result: The workspace opens with an IDE in your browser.

=== Step 4: Open OpenCode Web UI

Your workspace has an OpenCode Web UI endpoint. Find it in:

* The DevSpaces dashboard → your workspace → *Endpoints* tab → `opencode-web`
* Or the IDE's port forwarding panel → port 4096

Open it in a new tab and enter the password from your credentials card.

Expected result: OpenCode chat interface loads. You see a prompt input at the
bottom.

=== Step 5: Verify model connectivity

In the OpenCode chat, type:

----
Hello, what model are you?
----

Expected result: The model responds with its name (a variant of
`{model_id}`). If you get a connection error, check with the facilitator.

=== Step 6: Verify the terminal

In the IDE, open a terminal (*Terminal* → *New Terminal* or `Ctrl+``).

[source,bash,role="execute"]
----
oc whoami
----

Expected result: Your username is printed. This confirms `oc` is authenticated.

[source,bash,role="execute"]
----
ls /projects/mcp-dev-workshop/.opencode/skills/
----

Expected result: You see `build-mcp-server` listed. The skill is ready.

[IMPORTANT]
====
If any step fails, do not proceed. Raise your hand or message the facilitator.
The workshop depends on a working workspace with model connectivity.
====
